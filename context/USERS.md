# USERS

Status: ACTIVE

## Evidence labels
- **Reported:** what a participant told me.
- **Observed:** what I saw directly.
- **Inferred:** my interpretation, not something the participant said.

Two exploratory interviews do not establish how common these patterns
are. Each interview lasted about 15 minutes and asked about one specific
recent experience. I did not record raw notes in this repository.

## Interview synthesis

### INT-01
INT-01 was an interview with Maya, a business student, about a recent
four-person operations presentation. She reported that two sections
looked active in the deck but lacked evidence, citations, or alignment
with the rest of the presentation. She reported that she and a teammate
repaired those sections late on Sunday and that the repair cost the team
its rehearsal time. She also described the team as having defined
"finished" differently. I did not observe the deck or the team's
messages; this evidence comes from the interview only. I inferred that
visible activity gave the team false confidence and that polished slides
may have hidden weak understanding. This interview supports my
assumption that early finishers end up repairing others' work. It leaves
uncertain whether she meets this pattern across multiple courses,
because I asked about one project.

### INT-02
INT-02 was an interview with Daniel, a working student, about a recent
marketing case presentation. He reported that he researched competitors
but left out citations and pricing because the team had no completion
criteria. He reported that the gap surfaced at the internal deadline and
that a teammate replaced his work. I did not observe his section or the
team's messages; this evidence comes from the interview only. I inferred
that reminders alone would not have fixed the misunderstanding, that his
outside research may have looked like inactivity to teammates, and that
early review would have felt safer to him than replacement. This
interview supports my assumption that he needs clear expectations and
feedback before failure. It leaves uncertain whether he prefers
asynchronous clarification to extra meetings.

## Jobs

**JOB-01:** When I contribute a presentation section, I want to know the
evidence and integration standards, so I can deliver usable work without
causing unexpected repairs.
Evidence: INT-02 (missed citations and pricing because completion was
undefined); INT-01 (active-looking sections still needed evidence and
integration repairs).

**JOB-02:** When my team approaches a deadline, I want to see risks and
assign responsibility early, so we can protect rehearsal without
burdening early finishers.
Evidence: INT-01 (incomplete sections near the deadline, unassigned
repairs, lost rehearsal); INT-02 (gaps became visible too late for
normal feedback).

## Profiles

### Profile 1: Early-finishing integrator (INT-01)
- **Situation:** *Reported:* Maya finishes her part early and checks
  whether the full presentation fits together. *Assumed:* she
  encounters this pattern across multiple courses.
- **Job:** *Reported:* she needs to identify gaps early enough for the
  owner to respond. *Assumed:* she wants repair ownership recorded for
  later workload discussions.
- **Current approach:** *Reported:* she monitors the deck, messages
  teammates, and repairs gaps when time is short. *Assumed:* she treats
  slide activity as a progress signal.
- **Why unsatisfying:** *Reported:* activity conceals gaps, early
  finishers become editors, and rehearsal disappears. *Assumed:*
  repeated correction may damage trust.

### Profile 2: Time-constrained section owner (INT-02)
- **Situation:** *Reported:* Daniel balances coursework with part-time
  shifts and works in limited time blocks. *Assumed:* asynchronous
  clarification suits him better than extra meetings.
- **Job:** *Reported:* he needs clear expectations and feedback before
  failure. *Assumed:* he wants to request review without appearing
  unreliable.
- **Current approach:** *Reported:* he interprets requirements,
  researches, edits slides, and uses group messages. *Assumed:* he
  would use a checklist attached to his section.
- **Why unsatisfying:** *Reported:* invisible research, vague
  expectations, and late feedback waste effort. *Assumed:* replacement
  reduces his opportunity to learn through revision.

These profiles come from two exploratory interviews. They do not show
how common these patterns are.

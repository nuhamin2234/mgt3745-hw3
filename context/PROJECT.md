## Problem (candidate from HW1)
In an introductory marketing class, my four-person team divided a
presentation about a local coffee shop into customer research,
competitor analysis, pricing, and promotion. Two sections arrived the
night before the deadline without supporting evidence, leaving me and
another teammate to research missing information and combine the slides.
We submitted on time but lost our planned rehearsal. The problem is how
to produce a coherent presentation without making the teammates who
finish earlier automatically responsible for last-minute repairs.

## Why the problem is wicked
- **Hard to formulate:** I could call it unequal effort. A teammate
  could call it unclear expectations about evidence. Calling it
  procrastination assumes a cause before knowing whether everyone
  understood what "finished" required.
- **No stopping rule:** Submitting ended the assignment but did not show
  the collaboration was adequate. The deadline was an external stopping
  point.
- **Good or bad, not true or false:** Earlier deadlines protect
  rehearsal but burden someone with limited availability. Assigning all
  evidence checks to the strongest researcher improves quality but makes
  workload less fair.
- **Multiple valid explanations:** Missing evidence could reflect rushed
  work, uncertainty about credible sources, or an assumption that
  someone else would add citations. Each suggests a different response.
- **No immediate or ultimate test:** On-time submission does not prove
  the process worked, because two members absorbed unexpected work.

**Verdict:** Adding a missing citation is tame. Deciding how a team
should prevent and distribute repair work involves competing priorities.

## Three framings
- **A (obvious):** The team discovers incomplete sections too late to
  correct them before rehearsal.
- **B (stakeholder shift):** A teammate struggling to supply evidence
  has no agreed way to clarify expectations and request help before the
  section is due.
- **C (goal shift):** The team lacks a shared process for producing an
  evidence-supported, coherent presentation while protecting rehearsal
  time and distributing final repairs fairly.

## Chosen framing: C
Our goal was a presentation we could explain and defend together.
Separate sections were intermediate contributions. When we treated their
completion as the main milestone, combining arguments and checking
evidence became emergency work. Framing C makes integration and
rehearsal explicit team responsibilities instead of work inherited by
whoever finishes first.

**What this framing makes invisible:** Protecting the final presentation
can obscure individual learning, since assigning evidence checks to
experienced researchers denies others practice. It can also understate
personal constraints: a balanced-looking division may still be hard for
someone with limited availability.

## Oath check
The tool I already assumed was a shared spreadsheet tracking each
section, its owner, sources, and review status. It would be justified
only if missing progress information explained why we found the gaps
late, members updated it, and "complete" had a shared definition. If
teammates already knew about the gaps but lacked skills or time, the
spreadsheet would document the problem without addressing its cause.

## Adequacy sketch ("good enough for now")
For the next presentation: an evidence-supported combined draft 48 hours
before the deadline, one full rehearsal, and an explicit team agreement
assigning any remaining repairs. Check draft timestamps, source
coverage, rehearsal completion, and a brief workload review.
